Former residents of the Southern Woods, lingering dead who never left, spirits who found Bell's candles and came to listen because the living world forgot to hold them. They watch. They comment. They steal Bell's shawls. They critique her séance technique. They start haunted book clubs. They possess the toaster with alarming regularity and nobody has figured out why.

The ghosts are part of the house's resonance. Their presence is why the walls carry emotion — the dead understand grief better than the living because they've finished with it, and they lend that understanding to the architecture. When a girl cries in the communal bath, the vibration that reaches the corridor walls three rooms away is partly architectural acoustics and partly the dead holding the sound gently as it travels, softening it into warmth rather than noise.

Bell speaks to them. Regularly. Conversationally. She scolds them for stealing her shawls and they scold her for dramatic lighting choices and everyone pretends this is normal because in Banshee Haven it is.

## Seasonal Mood

**Winter** — The underground corridors hold warmth like cupped hands. The Grotto steams. The greenhouse glass frosts into patterns that Bell reads like omens and Elspeth ignores in favor of building the fire higher. The cottage kitchen produces soup in quantities that could feed the entire property, and frequently does — Ghost Haven and Specter Haven girls trekking through cold air to Banshee Haven's door because Elspeth's winter cooking is a gravitational force. Séances run longer. The candles burn lower. The ghosts are quieter in winter, as if even the dead need rest.

**Spring** — The greenhouse erupts. Whatever grows between Bell's breasts — between the Cathedral and the cottage — blooms with the particular fervor of a garden tended by ghosts and warmed by hot-tub steam. The fairy-lit pathways fill with pollen and the scent of things returning. Bell holds séances outdoors for the first time since fall, candles guttering in spring breezes, and the ghosts attend in greater numbers because the living world is waking up and the dead want to watch.

**Summer** — The Cathedral's underground corridors become the coolest space on the property, and girls from all three houses migrate to the Resonance Hall when the heat above ground becomes unbearable. The Grotto hosts late-night swims. The cottage windows open wide and Elspeth's kitchen spills baking smells into warm air. The greenhouse grows wild — untamed, heavy with life, the glass walls fogging from interior heat. Cross-house gatherings cluster around the fairy-lit pathways between the two buildings, girls sitting on the stone benches with bare legs and cold drinks and the easy intimacy of a family that has three houses and one heart.

**Fall** — Bell's season. The candles burn richer. The séances deepen. The air carries woodsmoke and the particular melancholy of leaves turning and the cottage filling with Elspeth's pumpkin bread and the Cathedral absorbing the grief that autumn loosens in every girl who survived St. Jonah's. The ghosts are most active in fall — restless, talkative, stealing more shawls than usual. The greenhouse turns amber and gold through its glass walls, and the fairy-lit pathways glow warmer against the cooling air. If Banshee Haven has a season, it's this one — the one where the world practices dying and the house holds space for it.

## Anchor Mood

Banshee Haven doesn't shout. It doesn't whisper either. It *keens.*

A banshee's cry is not horror — it's *grief made audible.* The sound a soul makes when it loves so much that losing becomes a frequency, a vibration, a note that carries through walls and corridors and the bones of anyone close enough to hear. That's what this house does. It takes the grief that girls carry — the buried kind, the quiet kind, the kind that doesn't brat or fuck or scheme its way out but just *sits* in the chest like a stone that no one ever taught you how to put down — and it gives that grief a voice.

Ghost Haven is the hearth that holds you.
Specter Haven is the church that fucks you holy.
Banshee Haven is the cry you finally let yourself make when someone loves you enough to listen.

Bell sits in her Séance Parlor at the apex of the Cathedral — at the nipple of the left breast, the most exposed point of the most vulnerable house in the Found Family — and she lights her candles, and the ghosts gather, and the living gather, and someone starts to cry, and the house *sings with them.* Not harmony. Resonance. The walls vibrating with the frequency of a girl discovering that her grief is not a burden but a language, and that someone in this house speaks it fluently.

And when the crying stops — when the candles gutter and the ghosts withdraw and the girl sits trembling in the aftermath of her own exorcism — Elspeth is waiting. With food. With blankets. With the particular steadiness of a woman who has made reconstruction into a discipline and a devotion and a way of living that says: *I don't need to understand your grief to hold you through it. I just need to be here when you're done.*

The land grew this house from tears the way it grew Ghost Haven from love and Specter Haven from desire. Not sadness — *catharsis.* The release that comes after holding. The exhale after the scream. The particular tenderness that only exists in the space after breaking, when the pieces are still warm and the hands that hold them are gentle and the light through the stained glass makes everything look like it was always meant to be this beautiful.

Banshee Haven is not the darkest house. It's the most *honest.* The house where no masks are allowed and no lies survive and every girl who enters knows, in the part of her that she hides from everyone including herself:

*This is where I can fall apart without being punished.*

And the house answers, in stone and glass and candle flame and the voice of a girl who speaks to the dead:

*Fall. I'll hold every piece.*
